# Current gaps and next implementation order

As of 2026-10-24, the browser VCF parser, eight-gene caller, three sample VCFs, other-format intake, and Maya VCF-driven CPIC demo lookup are working, but the project is **not a clinical interpretation service**.

1. **Real test result verification:** there is no lab identity check, assay quality/coverage validation, reference-call proof, or signed report. Self-declared JSON values remain `review_required` and never automatically reach `assess()`.
2. **Clinical validation of allele/phenotype calls:** Kai's `callStarAlleles` now calls eight genes using pinned PharmCAT 3.4.0 definitions and CPIC phenotype data, and its calls match three bundled PharmCAT examples. Those fixtures do not validate real patient assays. A clinical path still needs assay coverage/provenance, genotype quality and FILTER checks, phase resolution, build verification, and specialized outside calls for CYP2D6/HLA. The caller marks missing/no-call positions Indeterminate and assumed phase ambiguous. Uploaded VCFs produce research calls only.
3. **Clinical scope/review:** the eight target genes have 41 CPIC A/B gene–drug pair entries indexed, but these are research metadata, not personalized recommendations. Only CYP2C19 + clopidogrel in ACS/PCI has five demo guidance rows (Poor, Intermediate, Normal, Rapid, Ultrarapid Metabolizer). Some sample phenotypes are blank and some guidelines require multiple genes, including genes outside the target eight. Each additional guideline needs exact lookup-key, population and indication validation plus qualified review. The wording and source usage terms need qualified review. A normal result does not prove efficacy or safety.
   Research routing now identifies 134 exact source-row IDs for clopidogrel, thiopurines and selected statins, but stores no prescribing text. Warfarin requires a separate guideline algorithm; CYP2C9 statin keys can require an activity score. These routes cannot be shown as patient advice until the caller, source terms, and clinical wording are reviewed. See [research handoff](./PHENOTYPE_TO_GUIDANCE_RESEARCH.md).
4. **Drug identity:** the research catalog now has 34 exact generic ingredients and 60 current U.S. brand mappings from NLM Prescribable RxNorm, but no validated product/NDC normalizer or openFDA NDC route exists. A brand string may cover multiple strengths/forms or change over time; it cannot yet be used as a clinical join to CPIC's ingredient ID. Combination brands are excluded from this single-ingredient alias file.
   The `/drugs` page now browses 34 exact ingredients under nine editorial therapeutic areas, but it does not categorize patient response. Endocrine and Miscellaneous have no indexed drugs. Classification is derived from CPIC guideline families, not verified against RxClass or every indication, and can be incomplete for drugs used in more than one area.
   Imipramine and trimipramine have no brand in the current U.S. prescribable subset; lornoxicam and tenoxicam are absent from that subset entirely. Historical or non-U.S. names (for example Coumadin) are intentionally not listed as current U.S. brands. Refresh monthly and review changes before expanding search or making product claims.
5. **Clinician access and presentation:** no clinician portal, NPI lookup or real authentication exists. NPI alone would be demo access only. Full CPIC recommendation text is not exposed on the patient page.
6. **Source operations:** the CPIC candidate fetch is manual. A multi-guideline research routing schema now exists, but no scheduled refresh, provenance diff UI, source-change alert, or clinically reviewed multi-guideline guidance snapshot exists. The validator checks selected fields but not every clinical qualifier or terms change.
7. **Privacy/security:** browser-only local parsing avoids upload but does not establish production privacy compliance, threat modeling, consent, retention policy, accessibility audit, or lab/system integration.
8. **Evidence boundaries:** openFDA adverse events, PharmVar allele definitions, FDA labeling, CDC GeT-RM and population datasets are documented research inputs only. They cannot be treated as independent patient-response recommendations.

Suggested next increment: choose one real laboratory export format, create a de-identified fixture and field mapping, validate its provenance/coverage, then validate its caller evidence and route a clinician-reviewed phenotype into the existing CPIC lookup. In parallel, add RxNorm normalization and a clinician detail screen with real access controls before widening the source snapshot.
